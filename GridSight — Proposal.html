GridSight

India's AI power-readiness radar

26.3 GW projected AI data-center demand by 2032, against 6.5 GW currently planned

That gap is the whole pitch. Nobody has turned it into something you can point at, state by state, with sources attached.

The problem

India's AI data-center buildout is running into a wall of electricity, not chips or land. Every state runs its own data-center policy — different power guarantees, different renewable mandates, different incentives — published as scattered PDFs that nobody has stitched together. Companies and policymakers are making multi-hundred-crore siting decisions without a clear, shared view of where the grid will actually hold up.

The idea

An AI agent reads India's state data-center policies and grid-capacity reports, turns them into a structured, source-cited database, and combines that with public data-center pipeline announcements to project when each state's grid headroom runs out. Wrap that in a simple, explainable scorecard, and you have something no one else has built for India specifically — not a platform, one sharp tool that answers one real question well.

The extraction agent

Reads each state's policy PDF and grid report, pulls out structured fields — power guarantee, renewable mandate, land subsidy, MW target — and attaches the exact source page to every value. This is the real engineering, not a chatbot reading back its own database.

The power-ceiling forecast

Sets each state's announced data-center pipeline against its installed-plus-committed grid capacity, and projects the year that state's headroom for new AI facilities runs out. This is the one line people will repeat back to us.

The explainable scorecard

Adjustable weights, hard constraint gates — a high score can't hide a power shortfall — and a click-through trail from any number back to its source document. Trustworthy by design, not a black box.

The demo moment

Instead of walking judges through screens, we ask someone in the room to name any Indian state, live. GridSight pulls up its power-ceiling projection and policy comparison for that state on the spot, source document open alongside it. That's the moment that gets remembered.
